the NCAA

National Collegiate Athletic Association

What is the NCAA?

The NCAA is the main governing body for university sport in the United States. The organisation provides a structured pathway for athletes to combine university education with competitive sport.

Who is part of it?

The NCAA is made up of more than 1,000 university institutions across three divisions. These institutions compete in organised sporting competitions across a wide range of sports, including track and field, cross country, swimming, basketball, soccer and tennis.

What does it do?

The NCAA enables university teams to take student-athletes to competitions and to host NCAA-sanctioned meets. It also establishes and governs eligibility requirements, organises national championships and regulates how universities recruit and support student-athletes.

How do the divisions differ?

The three divisions offer different levels of competition, university environments, academic opportunities and scholarship structures. Understanding these differences is an important part of finding the right university and opportunity for each athlete.
